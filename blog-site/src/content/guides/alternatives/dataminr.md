## Start with the job you need to replace

A corporate security team has to connect an event to a facility, notify an owner, and keep a record of the response. A general intelligence reader may only need to understand what is happening. The handoff after an alert is a major part of the buying decision. Compare the required output, access, and handoff before comparing feature lists. The alternatives below solve different parts of the work.

## 1. World Monitor

**Best for public-source situational awareness.**

Start here when your deliverable is a briefing and the free core dashboard covers it. Budget separately for any paid capabilities. This is not an equivalent replacement for corporate alert routing or incident management.

Read [World Monitor](https://www.worldmonitor.app/dashboard) and check the required access before choosing.

## 2. Crisis24

**Best for travel risk and assistance.**

Consider Horizon when your requirement includes people visibility, mass notification, and assistance. Evaluate who responds to an incident and what the contracted service includes.

Read [Crisis24](https://www.crisis24.com/platforms/crisis24-horizon) and check the required access before choosing.

## 3. Recorded Future

**Best for combined physical and cyber intelligence.**

Evaluate its geopolitical and threat-intelligence modules when investigations need security context across domains. Ask which modules and integrations are included in the proposal.

Read [Recorded Future](https://www.recordedfuture.com/products/geopolitical-intelligence) and check the required access before choosing.

## When to keep Dataminr

Keep Dataminr if your security operation depends on asset monitoring and documented response workflows. World Monitor can be an additional research view without replacing that system. Its [official product documentation](https://www.dataminr.com/products/corporate-security/) describes the original scope. Its [facility monitoring](https://www.dataminr.com/products/pulse/corporate-security/facility-and-event-security/) and [operational resilience](https://www.dataminr.com/products/pulse/corporate-security/operational-resilience/) pages describe those workflows.

## Our pick

For corporate event detection and incident workflows, evaluate Dataminr first. For the narrower task of a broad public-source overview, try World Monitor's free core dashboard. For a different operational requirement, evaluate the specialist listed above. Avoid buying breadth that your team will not use.

## What to do before switching

Inventory every downstream alert recipient, escalation rule, and reporting obligation. Run a parallel evaluation with representative locations. Verify that a real operator can receive, acknowledge, and act on a test incident. Keep the current workflow until the replacement passes those checks.

Compare the full cost of the workflow, including paid access and the analyst time needed to fill gaps. Do not cancel a working service just because a free dashboard overlaps with one screen.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there a free Dataminr alternative?**

World Monitor's core dashboard is free and can support public-source situational awareness. It is not a feature-for-feature replacement for Dataminr. Define the task first, then check whether free access is enough.

**Can I use more than one tool?**

Yes. A specialist tool can supply the primary research or operational workflow while World Monitor supplies wider context. Keep the original provider's source attribution and access terms.

**What should a migration prove?**

The replacement must produce your required output with acceptable coverage and a working handoff. Save a representative example and document every requirement that still needs another service.
