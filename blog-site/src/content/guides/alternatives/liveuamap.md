## Start with the job you need to replace

If your entire assignment is to follow developments in one theater, a focused map can be easier to scan than a dashboard covering many subjects. If your brief also asks about nearby transport, infrastructure, and market conditions, switching between separate tools adds work. These are different information needs. Compare the required output, access, and handoff before comparing feature lists. The alternatives below solve different parts of the work.

## 1. World Monitor

**Best for a broad public-source briefing.**

Use it when the map is one part of a larger briefing that includes transport, infrastructure, and markets. You gain a shared dashboard, but must still verify individual reports. It does not reproduce Liveuamap's editorial feed.

Read [World Monitor](https://www.worldmonitor.app/dashboard) and check the required access before choosing.

## 2. ACLED

**Best for a consistent conflict dataset.**

Choose ACLED when you need defined event categories and research-ready records rather than a stream of mapped news. Access, reporting lag, and data use terms matter. This is a change of research method, not a faster news feed.

Read [ACLED](https://acleddata.com/conflict-data/knowledge-base/methodology) and check the required access before choosing.

## 3. Dataminr

**Best for corporate facility monitoring.**

Evaluate Dataminr when the actual goal is to detect disruptive events near business locations and route them into a security workflow. A public map alone does not provide that operating process.

Read [Dataminr](https://www.dataminr.com/products/corporate-security/) and check the required access before choosing.

## When to keep Liveuamap

Stay with Liveuamap when your team already uses its regional reporting to follow a conflict. A broader interface alone is not a reason to replace that workflow. Its [official product documentation](https://dc.liveuamap.com/about) describes the original scope.

## Our pick

For following a specific conflict through mapped reports, evaluate Liveuamap first. For the narrower task of a broad public-source overview, try World Monitor's free core dashboard. For a different operational requirement, evaluate the specialist listed above. Avoid buying breadth that your team will not use.

## What to do before switching

Write down the regions and event categories you rely on today. Run both tools through the same reporting week. Keep links to original reports in your brief so a changed map does not erase your evidence. If your audience expects a timeline, prove that the replacement can support that timeline before switching.

Compare the full cost of the workflow, including paid access and the analyst time needed to fill gaps. Do not cancel a working service just because a free dashboard overlaps with one screen.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there a free Liveuamap alternative?**

World Monitor's core dashboard is free and can support public-source situational awareness. It is not a feature-for-feature replacement for Liveuamap. Define the task first, then check whether free access is enough.

**Can I use more than one tool?**

Yes. A specialist tool can supply the primary research or operational workflow while World Monitor supplies wider context. Keep the original provider's source attribution and access terms.

**What should a migration prove?**

The replacement must produce your required output with acceptable coverage and a working handoff. Save a representative example and document every requirement that still needs another service.
