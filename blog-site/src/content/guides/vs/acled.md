This guide is published by World Monitor. We compare public product documentation and our own documented capabilities. Recommendations are editorial judgments, not a hands-on benchmark. Sources were checked on October 2, 2026. Confirm current access and contracts before buying.

## TL;DR

> ACLED is the better choice for conflict-event research. World Monitor is the better starting point for a broad daily dashboard. A dashboard view does not replace ACLED data access or its methodology.

## The comparison at a glance

| Decision | World Monitor | ACLED |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main workflow | Daily situational overview | Structured conflict-event analysis |
| Event definitions | Depend on each underlying source | Published coding methodology |
| Historical research | Verify available source and time range | Evaluate original dataset access |
| Other domains | News, transport, markets, and infrastructure context | Conflict and protest specialization |
| Access | Core dashboard free; premium access varies | myACLED level determines data granularity and timing |
| Best fit | Monitoring and finding questions | Research that needs comparable events |

## An event dataset or an intelligence overview?

A researcher needs consistent event definitions, date ranges, and a record of dataset revisions. A daily analyst may instead need to notice a development and find the original report. Counting dashboard markers cannot establish a research dataset with a defined denominator.

## Where ACLED is better

ACLED publishes a conflict-event methodology and an access framework for its data and tools. That makes it our recommendation for work where event classification and comparability matter. Use the original records and methodological notes when the question concerns event counts or changes over time. See the [official ACLED documentation](https://acleddata.com/conflict-data/knowledge-base/methodology). Review the [myACLED access table](https://acleddata.com/myacled-faqs) before relying on event-level or API access.

## Where World Monitor fits better

World Monitor combines conflict context with news and adjacent global signals. That helps a generalist decide where to look next. It is useful before a deeper research step, but seeing a third-party dataset through a dashboard does not transfer the provider's full archive, permissions, or research contract. See the [World Monitor overview](/blog/posts/what-is-worldmonitor-real-time-global-intelligence/) and [current plans](https://www.worldmonitor.app/pro#pricing) before assuming a capability is included.

## The limits that matter

ACLED records reflect reported events and a defined coding process, not every event that occurs. Its access levels differ. Do not equate Open myACLED with unrestricted, current event-level exports. World Monitor's available conflict layers can also differ from the complete source dataset.

## Our pick

Keep ACLED for event-level research, reproducible analysis, or a project that depends on its definitions. World Monitor can complement that work, but is not a substitute for the original dataset. Choose [World Monitor](https://www.worldmonitor.app/dashboard) when the narrower job is a broad public-source briefing and its available sources answer your question.

## What to do today

Define one research question, event category, and date range. Check whether your myACLED access provides the records you need. Separately use World Monitor to prepare the current regional context. If the final output contains statistical claims, trace those claims to the original dataset rather than the dashboard.

Keep the evidence behind the decision. Save a sample brief, its source links, and the requirements that the tool did not meet. A polished map is not enough if your team still cannot produce the required output.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is World Monitor a full replacement for ACLED?**

No. It can cover a public-source overview, but ACLED is the stronger fit for research with consistently coded conflict events. Evaluate the required workflow rather than assuming all intelligence products are equivalent.

**Which option costs less?**

World Monitor has a free core dashboard. Premium and developer capabilities have separate access terms. Check ACLED's current offering before comparing the cost of the complete workflow. This guide does not invent a competitor price or assume every feature is free.

**Did you measure which tool is faster?**

No. This is a documentation-based comparison. Evaluate source timestamps, gaps, and the action your team can take. We do not claim a measured latency or accuracy advantage.
