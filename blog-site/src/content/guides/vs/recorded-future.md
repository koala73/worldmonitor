This guide is published by World Monitor. We compare public product documentation and our own documented capabilities. Recommendations are editorial judgments, not a hands-on benchmark. Sources were checked on October 2, 2026. Confirm current access and contracts before buying.

## TL;DR

> Recorded Future is the better choice for a security team that needs threat investigations and enterprise intelligence integrations. World Monitor is a better starting point for an accessible public-source global overview. Both offer agent-facing capabilities; compare scope and access rather than assuming MCP is unique to World Monitor.

## The comparison at a glance

| Decision | World Monitor | Recorded Future |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main workflow | Broad global monitoring | Enterprise security intelligence |
| Cyber investigation | Public context and available feeds | Dedicated threat-intelligence module |
| Geopolitical work | Cross-domain map and news overview | Facility threats and country-risk monitoring |
| AI agents | Documented World Monitor MCP interface | Recorded Future also documents MCP tools |
| Access | Free core dashboard; paid developer features vary | Confirm module, API, and MCP entitlement |
| Best fit | Generalist research and contextual applications | Security investigations and integrations |

## A broad public dashboard or a security intelligence program?

An analyst checking world events needs a different tool from a threat hunter investigating attacker infrastructure. Security intelligence also has to enter existing controls and analyst workflows. A large collection of public map layers does not answer those operational questions by itself.

## Where Recorded Future is better

Recorded Future documents a Geopolitical Intelligence module for physical threats and country risk, and a separate Threat Intelligence module for cyber investigations. Its published integrations matter when your organization already works through security tools. Evaluate the modules you actually need rather than the brand as a single bundle. See the [official Recorded Future documentation](https://www.recordedfuture.com/products/geopolitical-intelligence). Read the [Threat Intelligence module](https://www.recordedfuture.com/products/threat-intelligence) and [Recorded Future MCP tools](https://support.recordedfuture.com/hc/en-us/articles/55777121249043-Recorded-Future-MCP-Available-Tools) for those separate capabilities.

## Where World Monitor fits better

World Monitor brings broad public-source context into a browser dashboard and offers developer APIs and an MCP interface. It suits a generalist who wants to explore global signals or build a contextual briefing. Access varies by product and plan. It does not claim the same threat-investigation depth as Recorded Future. See the [World Monitor overview](/blog/posts/what-is-worldmonitor-real-time-global-intelligence/) and [current plans](https://www.worldmonitor.app/pro#pricing) before assuming a capability is included.

## The limits that matter

This comparison does not measure repository size, threat-detection accuracy, or investigation quality. We have not tested either MCP service against a common task. Having an agent interface says little about source coverage, retrieval permissions, or whether an answer preserves its evidence.

## Our pick

Keep Recorded Future when your security program relies on its threat intelligence, investigations, or integrations. Use World Monitor as adjacent geopolitical context if that helps the analyst. Choose [World Monitor](https://www.worldmonitor.app/dashboard) when the narrower job is a broad public-source briefing and its available sources answer your question.

## What to do today

Choose one physical-risk question and one threat investigation relevant to your team. Ask Recorded Future to show the required modules and integrations. Check the World Monitor API or MCP response against the same public-context question. Compare returned sources, timestamps, access limits, and how much manual work remains.

Keep the evidence behind the decision. Save a sample brief, its source links, and the requirements that the tool did not meet. A polished map is not enough if your team still cannot produce the required output.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is World Monitor a full replacement for Recorded Future?**

No. It can cover a public-source overview, but Recorded Future is the stronger fit for enterprise threat investigations and geopolitical exposure. Evaluate the required workflow rather than assuming all intelligence products are equivalent.

**Which option costs less?**

World Monitor has a free core dashboard. Premium and developer capabilities have separate access terms. Check Recorded Future's current offering before comparing the cost of the complete workflow. This guide does not invent a competitor price or assume every feature is free.

**Did you measure which tool is faster?**

No. This is a documentation-based comparison. Evaluate source timestamps, gaps, and the action your team can take. We do not claim a measured latency or accuracy advantage.
