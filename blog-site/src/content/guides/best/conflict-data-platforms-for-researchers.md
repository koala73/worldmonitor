## TL;DR

> ACLED is our first choice for research that depends on coded conflict events. World Monitor helps identify questions and current context. Liveuamap helps find reported incidents, but is not an interchangeable research dataset.

## What matters for conflict researchers

A research tool must preserve the meaning of an observation. Event definitions, revisions, access, and time coverage affect every comparison. A visually dense map can look comprehensive while giving you no defensible denominator for a statistical claim.

## Different jobs need different tools

### A reproducible dataset

Check definitions, geography, date range, and the data version. Save the records and method that support your result.

### Current context

Monitoring helps you formulate a question. It cannot substitute for the dataset and sampling rules used to answer that question.

### Permitted access

Check granularity, lag, export rights, and API entitlement. A free visualization does not necessarily permit the research or redistribution you plan.

## 1. ACLED

**Best for event-level conflict research.**

ACLED is the stronger option when you need a published coding method and comparable event records. Its myACLED framework distinguishes access levels. Confirm that your institution receives the records and timing the project requires. The existence of a public map is not evidence of unrestricted exports.

[Official ACLED documentation](https://acleddata.com/conflict-data/knowledge-base/methodology).

## 2. World Monitor

**Best for finding research questions.**

Use World Monitor to notice a development and inspect related news or geographic context. Its cross-domain dashboard can help formulate a hypothesis. Move the statistical analysis to the original source dataset. World Monitor's visible layers are not a replacement for a versioned research extract.

[World Monitor overview](/blog/posts/what-is-worldmonitor-real-time-global-intelligence/).

## 3. Liveuamap

**Best for incident discovery for case work.**

Use a mapped report feed to identify incidents worth examining in a case study. Preserve the original report and establish your inclusion rules. We would not choose it over ACLED for a study that depends on standardized event counts.

[Official Liveuamap documentation](https://dc.liveuamap.com/about).

## Our pick

Choose ACLED for the research dataset, then use World Monitor for context if useful. If your project is qualitative incident reconstruction, Liveuamap can help discover reports. Make the evidence standard explicit before gathering material.

## What to do today

1. Write the question, unit of analysis, event categories, countries, and time range. Check whether the source can support each one.
2. Obtain a sample through the access level you will actually use. Record its version and reproduce one calculation before committing to the full project.
3. Test whether a definition change or reporting delay changes your conclusion. State those limits in the method rather than treating missing reports as zero events.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Open myACLED enough for event-level research?**

Not necessarily. ACLED's access table distinguishes aggregated and event data, publication timing, and API access. Verify your assigned level and the current terms before designing the analysis.

**Is the lowest-cost option always the best choice?**

No. A free core dashboard can be enough for a public-source brief. A specialized dataset or operating service can be worth its cost when it supplies something the job requires. Compare the full workflow and current access rather than assuming equal scope.

**How were these recommendations made?**

We compared documented capabilities with the tasks in this guide. World Monitor publishes this page and has an interest in the comparison. We did not run a common vendor benchmark or test service response times.

For ACLED, check the [myACLED access table](https://acleddata.com/myacled-faqs) alongside its methodology.
