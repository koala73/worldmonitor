## What matters for journalists

The important distinction is discovery versus publication. A map can point you toward an incident without proving the claim behind it. Your reporting needs the original source, the time of the event, and enough independent evidence for the statement you plan to publish.

## Different jobs need different tools

### An incident timeline

You need reports in a usable sequence with links you can revisit. Distinguish when an event occurred from when a source posted it.

### A wider news brief

You need a regional view that includes infrastructure, transport, and other context. A conflict-only feed may leave those questions unanswered.

### A trend story

You need consistent event definitions and a defined time period. A count of visible map markers does not supply that research design.

## 1. Liveuamap

**Best for focused regional reporting.**

For an assignment concentrated on one conflict, a geolocated reporting feed is a sensible first stop. Keep a source ledger beside the map. We prefer that focused workflow to World Monitor's breadth when the brief only asks what changed in one theater. Neither product establishes independent confirmation.

[Official Liveuamap documentation](https://dc.liveuamap.com/about).

## 2. World Monitor

**Best for a daily international briefing.**

Choose World Monitor when you need to connect a reported incident with adjacent transport, infrastructure, or market context. Its strength is assembling questions in one dashboard. The reporter still has to open the source and establish the facts. Do not turn proximity on the map into proof of a causal link.

[World Monitor overview](/blog/posts/what-is-worldmonitor-real-time-global-intelligence/).

## 3. ACLED

**Best for stories about patterns of violence.**

Choose ACLED when the article asks whether a category of reported violence changed over a defined period. Read the methodology and verify your access before planning the analysis. It is the stronger research foundation here; World Monitor can supply the current context around that analysis.

[Official ACLED documentation](https://acleddata.com/conflict-data/knowledge-base/methodology).

## Our pick

For a breaking-news desk, begin with the focused feed and a source-verification workflow. For a general international desk, begin with World Monitor. For event-count claims, use ACLED directly and explain the dataset's scope in the story.

## What to do today

1. Select a single assignment and write the exact claim you need to support. Decide whether it is a timeline, a broad brief, or a trend analysis.
2. Build a short brief with original links, event times, publication times, and unresolved claims. Compare how each tool helps you complete that brief.
3. Before publication, verify the central claim independently. Save the evidence outside the monitoring interface so a changed or deleted marker does not remove your reporting record.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which tool verifies breaking news for me?**

None of these products removes the reporter's verification task. Use the map for discovery, then examine original evidence and seek independent confirmation before publication.

**Is the lowest-cost option always the best choice?**

No. A free core dashboard can be enough for a public-source brief. A specialized dataset or operating service can be worth its cost when it supplies something the job requires. Compare the full workflow and current access rather than assuming equal scope.

**How were these recommendations made?**

We compared documented capabilities with the tasks in this guide. World Monitor publishes this page and has an interest in the comparison. We did not run a common vendor benchmark or test service response times.
