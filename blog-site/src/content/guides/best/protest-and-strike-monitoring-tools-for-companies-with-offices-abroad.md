## TL;DR

> Warning of a protest or strike comes in two forms. Notice of planned events, such as an announced general strike next week, comes from analysts or editors who track them: choose Factal or Crisis24. Notice of unrest as it is reported comes from monitoring: choose World Monitor when a small team wants a free live map with a protest layer and country-scoped alerts to Slack, Microsoft Teams, email or a signed webhook at a published price, without a sales call. Choose International SOS when the job is alerting and assisting the employees affected. Use ACLED to measure protest trends, not to be warned.

## What matters for a company with offices abroad

Three questions separate these products:

- **Do you need to know before it starts?** A transport strike announced for Thursday is knowable on Monday, but only if someone tracks announcements. That is editorial work.
- **How is your exposure defined?** "Our twelve countries" fits a country-scoped rule. "Within two kilometres of the Lagos office" needs geofencing around an address.
- **Who acts on the warning?** A security lead reading Slack needs a link to the report. A travel manager needs to know which employees are there.

## 1. Factal

**Best for forward-looking notice of planned events.**

[Factal](https://www.factal.com/) pairs automated detection with a newsroom of editors who verify incidents, and it describes forward-looking insights on planned events and geopolitical developments near your assets, written by those editors. That is the closest match to wanting notice before a strike or demonstration begins. It is sold at a [flat fee per team](https://www.factal.com/roi/) with no published price.

## 2. Crisis24

**Best when unrest alerts sit beside traveler and site duty of care.**

[Crisis24 Horizon](https://www.crisis24.com/platforms/crisis24-horizon) describes alerts to threats and disruptions worldwide from a team of more than 200 analysts backed by automated detection, with analysis from regional experts. Choose it when the same team owns traveler tracking, mass notification and assistance. Confirm in the contract which countries, alert types and response services are included.

## 3. World Monitor

**Best for a small team that wants its own country watch running today.**

The [free map](https://www.worldmonitor.app/dashboard) needs no account and has a protest layer beside conflict, outages, aviation and shipping. On a paid plan, alert rules filter by country and severity (all, high or critical) and deliver to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email, browser push or a webhook signed with HMAC-SHA256, in real time or as a daily, twice-daily or weekly digest. Real-time alerts link to the original report ([notifications reference](https://www.worldmonitor.app/docs/api-notifications)). Developers can read the same unrest rows by country through the API described on the [incident feed page](https://www.worldmonitor.app/docs/incident-feed). Prices are published at [worldmonitor.app/pro](https://www.worldmonitor.app/pro#pricing).

The limits matter here. There is no protest-only or strike-only alert rule: a country rule sends the news alerts that meet your severity setting, and unrest stories arrive among them. There is no calendar of planned strikes or demonstrations. The protest layer on worldmonitor.app is built from media reports of about the last two hours and marks where unrest is being reported, not a verified event. Alerts are scoped by country, not by a radius around an office. There are no analysts and no assistance service.

The [civil unrest workflow](https://www.worldmonitor.app/use-cases/monitor-civil-unrest/) shows how to turn a protest report into a location brief.

## 4. International SOS

**Best when the question is which employees are affected and who helps them.**

[International SOS](https://www.internationalsos.com/services/travel-risk-management) describes real-time threat monitoring, alerts to impacted employees and 24/7 medical and security support, with civil unrest near a traveler's hotel as one of its scenarios. It is a managed duty-of-care service. Buy it for the response, not as a feed for your own analysts.

## 5. ACLED

**Best for measuring protest activity, not for being warned.**

[ACLED](https://acleddata.com/conflict-data/knowledge-base/methodology) sources and codes political violence and protest events through a weekly coding and review process. Use it to compare protest frequency across your countries or to brief leadership on a trend. Access and timing depend on your [myACLED access level](https://acleddata.com/myacled-faqs).

## Pick World Monitor when

- **You define exposure by country.** Rules take a list of countries and a severity level.
- **You want it running this week.** The map is free, and paid plans are self-serve at published prices.
- **Your team works in chat.** Alerts reach Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email, browser push or a signed webhook, with a link to the report.
- **Unrest is one of several things you watch.** Outages, airport disruption, shipping and conflict are on the same map.
- **You can do your own verification.** World Monitor shows what is being reported and by how many publishers; your team confirms local conditions.

## Our pick

If you need to know about a strike or demonstration before it starts, pick Factal, or Crisis24 when you also need traveler and site services. If a small team wants to watch its countries today and be told when serious unrest is reported, pick World Monitor. If the job is reaching and assisting employees, pick International SOS. Use ACLED to measure trends.

## What to do today

1. List the countries where you have offices and decide whether country scope is enough or you need a radius around each site.
2. Open the free World Monitor map, turn on the protest layer and check those countries. The layer shows about the last two hours and keeps no history, so look again through the day and save what you find.
3. If you need planned-event notice, ask Factal and Crisis24 to show last month's forward-looking notes for one of your countries.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can any tool predict a protest?**

Announced events can be tracked: unions and organizers publish dates. Spontaneous unrest cannot be reliably predicted. Products with editors or analysts report planned events; monitoring tools report unrest once it is in the news.

**Does World Monitor send protest or strike alerts?**

Not as a separate alert type. A paid alert rule for a country sends the news alerts that meet your severity setting, which include unrest stories, with a link to the original report. The free map's protest layer shows where unrest is being reported.

We compared documented capabilities with the tasks in this guide. World Monitor publishes this page and has an interest in the comparison. We did not run a common vendor benchmark or test response times.
