## What matters for students

A useful student tool teaches the difference between a source, an observation, and an interpretation. You need something you can access for the course, cite in an assignment, and inspect without buying an enterprise package. A source list matters more than a dashboard score you cannot explain.

## Different jobs need different tools

### A first monitoring exercise

Use an accessible overview with links to original reports. Limit the assignment to one region so you can check the evidence carefully.

### A research methods exercise

Read a dataset's definitions before calculating a trend. Learn why two tools can show different totals without either one proving the other wrong.

### A reporting exercise

Keep an incident timeline with publication and event times. Explain uncertainty instead of assuming a mapped report is confirmed.

## 1. World Monitor

**Best for a first global-intelligence dashboard.**

The free core dashboard is a practical starting point for a class briefing. It lets students explore news and available geographic signals in one workspace. Keep paid and developer capabilities separate when planning an assignment. Broad access does not mean every data source is complete or equally current.

[World Monitor overview](/blog/posts/what-is-worldmonitor-real-time-global-intelligence/).

## 2. ACLED

**Best for learning conflict-data methods.**

ACLED's published methodology is useful even before you obtain a research dataset. Its definitions help explain how event categories differ. Check the access level available through your institution. ACLED is a better fit than World Monitor when the assignment requires a coded event dataset.

[Official ACLED documentation](https://acleddata.com/conflict-data/knowledge-base/methodology).

## 3. Liveuamap

**Best for a regional report timeline.**

Choose Liveuamap when the exercise is to follow reports from a specific conflict and investigate their sources. Keep the scope narrow and do not infer a complete event count from the feed. It is more focused than a cross-domain dashboard for this particular task.

[Official Liveuamap documentation](https://dc.liveuamap.com/about).

## Our pick

Use World Monitor for the first briefing exercise, ACLED for data methods, and Liveuamap for a regional timeline. An enterprise risk product is usually unnecessary unless the course has a specific operational requirement and access arrangement.

## What to do today

1. Choose one region and one question that can be answered in a short briefing. Record what you already know before opening the dashboard.
2. Find several relevant reports and open their original sources. Note event time, publication time, location confidence, and what remains unknown.
3. Write a brief that separates observed facts from your interpretation. Ask a classmate to trace the central claim through your source links.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a paid intelligence platform for an OSINT course?**

Often a free dashboard and public methodological resources are enough for an introductory exercise. Check the actual assignment. Paid data may be needed for a specific export, time range, or permitted use.

**Is the lowest-cost option always the best choice?**

No. A free core dashboard can be enough for a public-source brief. A specialized dataset or operating service can be worth its cost when it supplies something the job requires. Compare the full workflow and current access rather than assuming equal scope.

**How were these recommendations made?**

We compared documented capabilities with the tasks in this guide. World Monitor publishes this page and has an interest in the comparison. We did not run a common vendor benchmark or test service response times.
