_Methodology maintained by [Elie Habib](https://www.worldmonitor.app/blog/authors/elie-habib/), founder of World Monitor. Published revisions are recorded in the [corrections log](/corrections)._

## Start here

Every few days a Sentinel-1 radar satellite passes over each watched land site, day or
night and through cloud. WorldMonitor reads summary statistics of each new radar scene over
a small box around the site and compares them with earlier scenes taken from exactly the same
orbit. When a scene differs from that baseline by more than its normal spread and by more
than a fixed floor, the site's flag is raised. The next scene from the same orbit then
either confirms the change or retracts it.

A confirmed flag says only that **imagery statistics changed**. It does not say what changed
or why. Flooding, harvest, snow, a drained reservoir, new construction and demolition can all
move radar statistics. Read the flag as a prompt to look at the scenes, not as a finding.

The flag is in a shadow period. During it, flags are computed and stored for review but are
not shown on site evidence, country summaries or the API.

## What is measured

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Archive | Microsoft Planetary Computer, collection `sentinel-1-grd` (Copernicus Sentinel data, free and open) |
| Footprint | A box of about 2 km on a side (±0.01° latitude) centred on the site |
| Statistics | Median and 98th percentile of each polarization band (VV and VH for most of the world) |
| Units | Decibels, 20·log10 of the ground-range amplitude |
| Sites | Watched land sites only; ports, chokepoints and other maritime sites are never scored |

A scene with less than 90% valid pixels over the footprint is no observation. A band whose
statistic is missing is skipped, never read as zero. Neither moves the flag.

## Comparable scenes

Radar backscatter depends strongly on viewing geometry, so a scene is compared only with
scenes that share all of:

- the constellation (Sentinel-1; its spacecraft share one baseline)
- the relative orbit
- the pass direction (ascending or descending)
- the acquisition mode and polarization set

A change of acquisition mode starts a new baseline. A scene from another orbit can neither
confirm nor retract a flag raised on this one. A site with no scene on an orbit for 180 days
drops that orbit.

## Baseline and change test

For each band statistic the baseline holds the newest 12 comparable scenes. It needs 6 before
any scene is scored; until then the site is **building its baseline**. The centre is the
median and the spread is a robust standard deviation (1.4826 × median absolute deviation),
never less than 0.25 dB.

A scene changes a statistic when its difference from the median is at least 3 spreads **and**
at least 1.5 dB.

| State | Next comparable scene | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Building baseline | any | added to the baseline; monitoring once 6 scenes are held |
| Monitoring | no statistic changed | added to the baseline |
| Monitoring | a statistic changed | **raised**; the scene is held aside, not added |
| Raised | the same statistic changed the same way | **confirmed**; the baseline restarts from the two changed scenes |
| Raised | no statistic changed | **retracted**; the held scene is dropped and the new one added |
| Raised | a different change | the newer change replaces the held one |
| Confirmed | any | added to the new baseline; monitoring once 6 scenes are held |

A site's state is the most significant state of its orbits: confirmed, then raised, then
monitoring, then building its baseline.

When a site is first scored, its baselines are filled from up to 6 archived scenes per orbit
from the previous 120 days, so it starts monitoring without waiting for new passes. Each daily
run spends a bounded share of its time on statistics requests; a site it does not reach keeps
its flag and is scored on a later run from the scenes still in the 30-day archive window.

## Shadow period and thresholds

The thresholds above were set from the spread of real scene statistics, and they will be
revised from the shadow period's distribution. The shadow period lasts at least 30 days and
until at least 30 raised flags have been confirmed or retracted. Before flags are shown, a
replay of 90 days over 30 sites is reviewed by hand and each confirmed flag is labelled as
environmental or not.

Observed during development, over 90 days ending 13 November 2026 on a spread sample of
watched land sites, a typical comparable scene differed from its baseline median by a few
tenths of a decibel. Coastal and river sites showed much wider spread from water surface
roughness, and most of their raised flags were retracted by the next scene.

## Limits

- The statistic is a summary of a 2 km box. A change confined to a few buildings can leave
  the median and the 98th percentile unchanged.
- Seasonal change (crops, snow, soil moisture, water level) is the most common cause of a
  confirmed flag. The flag does not attempt to remove it.
- Sentinel-1 does not image every site on every orbit. Some sites have one orbit only, and a
  few have none in the archive.
