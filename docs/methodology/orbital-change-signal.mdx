_Methodology maintained by [Elie Habib](https://www.worldmonitor.app/blog/authors/elie-habib/), founder of World Monitor. Published revisions are recorded in the [corrections log](/corrections)._

## Start here

Every few days a Sentinel-1 radar satellite passes over each watched land site, day or
night and through cloud. WorldMonitor reads summary statistics of each new radar scene over
a small box around the site and compares them with earlier scenes taken from exactly the same
orbit. When a scene differs from that baseline by more than its normal spread and by more
than a fixed floor, the site's flag is raised. The next scene from the same orbit then
either confirms the change or retracts it.

A confirmed flag says only that **imagery statistics changed**. It does not say what changed
or why. Flooding, harvest, snow, a drained reservoir, new construction and demolition can all
move radar statistics. Read the flag as a prompt to look at the scenes, not as a finding.

The flag is in a shadow period. During it, flags are computed and stored for review but are
not shown on site evidence, country summaries or the API.

## What is measured

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Archive | Microsoft Planetary Computer, collection `sentinel-1-grd` (Copernicus Sentinel data, free and open) |
| Footprint | A box of about 2 km on a side (±0.01° latitude) centred on the site |
| Statistics | Median and 98th percentile of each polarization band (VV and VH for most of the world) |
| Units | Decibels, 20·log10 of the ground-range amplitude |
| Sites | Watched land sites only; ports, chokepoints and other maritime sites are never scored |
| Left out | Water points (footprint more than 50% permanent water in ESA WorldCover 2021) and all but one site of a group whose footprints overlap |

A scene with less than 90% valid pixels over the footprint is no observation. A band whose
statistic is missing is skipped, never read as zero. Neither moves the flag. A response that
lacks a requested band altogether, or names it differently, is not read as no observation:
scoring stops for the run and the scene is read again later.

A scene whose statistics request fails on three runs in a row is skipped and counted, so one
scene the archive cannot serve does not hold its site back. In the replay sample below, 22 of
881 scenes (2.5%) returned HTTP 500 on every attempt.

## Comparable scenes

Radar backscatter depends strongly on viewing geometry, so a scene is compared only with
scenes that share all of:

- the constellation (Sentinel-1; its spacecraft share one baseline)
- the relative orbit
- the pass direction (ascending or descending)
- the acquisition mode and polarization set

A change of acquisition mode starts a new baseline. A scene from another orbit can neither
confirm nor retract a flag raised on this one. A site with no scene on an orbit for 180 days
drops that orbit.

## Baseline and change test

For each band statistic the baseline holds the newest 12 comparable scenes. It needs 6 before
any scene is scored; until then the site is **building its baseline**. The centre is the
median and the spread is a robust standard deviation (1.4826 × median absolute deviation),
never less than 0.25 dB.

A scene changes a statistic when its difference from the median is at least 3 spreads **and**
at least 1.5 dB.

| State | Next comparable scene | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Building baseline | any | added to the baseline; monitoring once 6 scenes are held |
| Monitoring | no statistic changed | added to the baseline |
| Monitoring | a statistic changed | **raised**; the scene is held aside, not added |
| Raised | the same statistic changed the same way | **confirmed**; the baseline restarts from the two changed scenes |
| Raised | no statistic changed | **retracted**; the held scene is dropped and the new one added |
| Raised | a different change | the newer change replaces the held one |
| Confirmed | any | added to the new baseline; monitoring once 6 scenes are held |

A site's state is the most significant state of its orbits: confirmed, then raised, then
monitoring, then building its baseline.

When a site is first scored, its baselines are filled from up to 6 archived scenes per orbit
from the previous 120 days, so it starts monitoring without waiting for new passes. A site
whose scoring did not finish is scored first on the next run, from the scenes still in the
30-day archive window.

## Capacity

Each daily run has 6 minutes. The site pass leaves change scoring at least 2 minutes, and
scoring then runs until the run ends, so it also gets whatever the site pass does not use.
Four sites are scored at once, and statistics requests start at most four a second.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Sites scored | 497 of 581 land sites (68 water points and 16 overlapping duplicates left out) |
| Orbits per site | 2.5 on average (replay sample) |
| New scenes per site | 0.34 a day (replay sample), about 170 statistics requests a day across all sites |
| Backfill | 6 scenes per orbit plus one search, about 16 requests per site, about 8,000 in all |
| Scoring capacity | 480 requests a run at the 2-minute floor, about 650 when the site pass takes its usual 3.3 minutes |
| Backfill time | about 17 days, 26 days at the floor |

The backfill finishes inside the 30-day shadow period. Scoring was
previously capped at 2 minutes of serial requests, about 160 a day, which is below the daily demand.

An archive refusal (HTTP 401, 403, 429 or 451) stops the run at the refused site. Sites
evaluated before it, and what scoring folded before it, are kept and published. The seeder then
backs off: 15 minutes after the first refusal, doubling for each refusal in a row, at most a
day, with no archive request in between.

## Shadow period and thresholds

The thresholds above were set from the spread of real scene statistics, and they will be
revised from the shadow period's distribution. The shadow period lasts at least 30 days and
until at least 30 raised flags have been confirmed or retracted, with fewer than one in ten of
them retracted. Each run checks this rule over every shadow flag record and logs where it
stands. Meeting it does not end the shadow: that is a reviewed code change. Before flags are
shown, a replay of 90 days over 30 sites is reviewed by hand and each confirmed flag is
labelled as environmental or not. When the shadow ends, each site continues from its shadow
flag record, so the shadow period's baselines and history are kept.

## Replay against the plan's targets

`node scripts/replay-orbital-change.mjs` replays recorded archive statistics through the
scorer. The sample is 90 days of Sentinel-1 scenes (16 August to 14 November 2026) at 30
watched land sites taken at a fixed stride through the registry, scored with no backfill.
24 of the 30 are still scored after water points and duplicates are left out.

| Measure | Plan target | All 30 sites | 24 scored sites |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raised flags | | 33 | 26 |
| Retracted share of resolved flags | under 1 in 10 | 18 of 21 | 13 of 15 |
| Confirmed flags at a site with 5% or more water | at most 1 in 4 | 2 of 3 | 1 of 2 |
| Known changes raised within the window after the event | at least half | 0 of 4 | 0 of 4 |

The environmental column is inferred from the water share of the footprint, not from a hand
review of the imagery. The known changes are the Kakhovka dam breach at Zaporizhzhia (30 days),
the start of the 2023 Gaza war (45 days), and the June 2025 strikes at Natanz and Fordow (30
days each). Zaporizhzhia raised five days before the breach and Gaza three months after the
event, so neither counts; Natanz and Fordow raised nothing.

No setting of the floor (1 to 3 dB), the spread multiplier (2 to 5) or the baseline size (6
or 8 scenes) meets the targets on this sample: the retracted share never falls below 0.67 and
at most one known change is raised in its window. The targets are therefore not met, and the
thresholds are left as they are rather than fitted to the sample. The shadow-exit rule keeps
the retraction target, so the flag stays in shadow until the scorer or the targets are revised.

Observed during development, over 90 days ending 13 November 2026 on a spread sample of
watched land sites, a typical comparable scene differed from its baseline median by a few
tenths of a decibel. Coastal and river sites showed much wider spread from water surface
roughness, and most of their raised flags were retracted by the next scene.

## Limits

- The statistic is a summary of a 2 km box. A change confined to a few buildings can leave
  the median and the 98th percentile unchanged.
- Seasonal change (crops, snow, soil moisture, water level) is the most common cause of a
  confirmed flag. The flag does not attempt to remove it.
- Sentinel-1 does not image every site on every orbit. Some sites have one orbit only, and a
  few have none in the archive.
