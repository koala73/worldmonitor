## Overview

Orbital Watch answers one question for any point on Earth: which satellites passed over it, and did any of them leave a public image? Right-click the map (or long-press on touch screens) and choose **Satellite passes here**. A sheet opens with every pass from the last 7 days and the next 72 hours, grouped by what the evidence supports.

A pass is not an image. Most satellites overhead carry no camera aimed at the point, many that do are not tasked to it, and some images are never published. Orbital Watch shows an image only when a public archive returns one.

## Method

### Passes from orbit elements

The [CelesTrak](https://celestrak.org) catalog provides general-perturbations orbit elements for each satellite. Your browser propagates them with the SGP4 model (satellite.js) in a background worker and finds the moments each satellite's footprint covered the point. When a satellite has several published element sets, each set handles the part of the window nearest its epoch, because one set propagated across days drifts.

### Sensor profiles

Each satellite maps to a sensor profile that says what it can see and what to expect afterwards:

| Profile field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Category | Open optical (Sentinel-2, Landsat), open radar (Sentinel-1), weather polar, commercial optical, commercial radar, military, crewed station, or geostationary |
| Footprint | The ground band the instrument covers, from its swath width or look and incidence angles |
| Coverage | Whether the mission images everywhere or only land and coast |
| Archive wait | How long the open archive usually takes to publish a scene: 24 hours for Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1, 72 hours for Landsat |
| Daily mosaic | Whether a daily global mosaic exists for the sensor |

Optical sensors need daylight, so an optical pass counts only when the sun is at least 5 degrees above the horizon at the point.

### Matching against archives

Your browser asks the public archives directly: Earth Search and Microsoft Planetary Computer for Sentinel-2, Sentinel-1 and Landsat scenes, NASA GIBS for daily mosaics and geostationary frames, and EUMETSAT for Meteosat frames. A scene belongs to a pass when its acquisition time is within 5 minutes of the computed pass. For optical scenes, Orbital Watch also reads the scene's cloud classification at the point itself, not just the scene-wide cloud percentage.

## Result tiers

Each pass gets exactly one tier. The tier says what the evidence supports, never more.

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `confirmed-image` | An open mission passed with the point in its footprint, in daylight for optical sensors, over ground the mission covers, and an archive holds a scene acquired within 5 minutes of the pass. The image is shown. |
| `likely-imaged` | The same conditions hold, but the archive has not answered yet or the archive wait has not passed. An image is probable, not shown. |
| `passed-overhead` with **not in archive** | The same conditions held, the archive wait has passed, and the archive has no matching scene. The mission was overhead and did not publish an image of the point. |
| `passed-overhead` | A crewed station was overhead. No image is implied. |
| `daily-mosaic` | A weather satellite in polar orbit covered the point, and its daily global mosaic for that day is shown when NASA GIBS has it. |
| `within-reach` | A commercial satellite passed with the point inside what it could have imaged. Commercial imagery is tasked and paid for, so nothing says an image was taken. |
| `capability-overhead` | A military satellite passed with the point in its footprint. Orbital Watch shows that the capability was overhead and nothing more. |
| `opportunity` | A future pass. It is a chance to image, never an image. |

Overhead or within reach never means an image was taken. Only `confirmed-image` and the daily mosaic and geostationary rows show imagery, and only imagery that a public archive returned.

## Accuracy limits

- **Orbit-element age.** SGP4 error grows with the time between a pass and the epoch of the elements it used. Each pass shows the age of its orbit data. Element sets older than 7 days are not used, so a satellite whose newest set is older gets no passes instead of a guess.
- **Swath-edge uncertainty.** A point within 5 km of a footprint edge is marked **Edge of swath**. Small timing or attitude differences can put it inside or outside the scene.
- **Cloud.** An optical scene can exist and still show only cloud. Each image says whether the point itself was clear, cloudy or in cloud shadow, or that only a scene-wide cloud figure is known.
- **Archive latency.** Open archives publish scenes hours to days after acquisition. Until the archive wait for a mission has passed, a pass with no scene stays `likely-imaged` rather than `passed-overhead` with not in archive.

## Change flags

Orbital Watch can compare a new image of a point with an earlier one and raise a change flag. The flag runs in shadow mode first. Flags are computed and stored for review, and no panel, risk score, alert, API endpoint or MCP tool shows them. Publication waits for at least 30 days of shadow running and at least 30 raised flags confirmed or retracted by review.

## Privacy

The point you pick stays in your browser. It goes only to the public archives your browser calls directly. A shared link carries the point rounded to two decimal places, about 1 km. Error reports and other telemetry have coordinate query parameters and coordinate path segments removed from every URL before they leave the browser.
