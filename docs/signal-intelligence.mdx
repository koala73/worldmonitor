## Signal Intelligence

The dashboard continuously analyzes data streams to detect significant patterns and anomalies. Signals appear in the header badge (⚡) with confidence scores.

For the canonical public methodology behind the news digest, story tracking,
daily briefing, LLM grounding, and bias controls, see
[News Digest and Briefing methodology](/methodology/news-digest-and-briefing).

### Intelligence Findings Badge

The header displays an **Intelligence Findings** badge that consolidates two types of alerts:

| Alert Type | Source | Examples |
|------------|--------|----------|
| **Correlation Signals** | Cross-source pattern detection | Velocity spikes, market divergence, prediction leading |
| **Unified Alerts** | Module-generated alerts | CII spikes, geographic convergence, infrastructure cascades |

**Interaction**: Clicking the badge—or clicking an individual alert—opens a detail modal showing:

- Full alert description and context
- Component breakdown (for composite alerts)
- Affected countries or regions
- Confidence score and priority level
- Timestamp and trending direction

This provides a unified command center for all intelligence findings, whether generated by correlation analysis or module-specific threshold detection.

### Signal Types

The system lists 14 distinct signal types across news, markets, military, and infrastructure domains:

**News & Source Signals**

| Signal | Trigger | What It Means |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| **◉ Convergence** | 3+ source types report same story within 30 minutes | Multiple independent channels confirming the same event—higher likelihood of significance |
| **△ Triangulation** | Wire + Government + Intel sources align | The "authority triangle"—when official channels, wire services, and defense specialists all report the same thing |
| **🔥 Velocity Spike** | Topic mention rate doubles with 6+ sources/hour | A story is accelerating rapidly across the news ecosystem |
| **📊 Keyword Spike** | Term frequency rises significantly above baseline | A keyword is breaking out across monitored sources—early indication of a developing story |

**Market Signals**

| Signal | Trigger | What It Means |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| **🔮 Prediction Leading** | Prediction market moves 5%+ with low news coverage | Markets pricing in information not yet reflected in news |
| **📰 News Leads Markets** | High news velocity without corresponding market move | Breaking news not yet priced in—potential mispricing |
| **✓ Market Move Explained** | Market moves 2%+ with correlated news coverage | Price action has identifiable news catalyst—entity correlation found related stories |
| **📊 Silent Divergence** | Market moves 2%+ with no correlated news after entity search | Unexplained price action after exhaustive search—possible insider knowledge or algorithm-driven |
| **📈 Sector Cascade** | Multiple related sectors moving in same direction | Market reaction cascading through correlated industries |

**Infrastructure & Energy Signals**

| Signal | Trigger | What It Means |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| **🛢 Flow Drop** | Pipeline flow disruption keywords detected | Physical commodity supply constraint—may precede price spike |
| **🔁 Flow-Price Divergence** | Pipeline disruption news without corresponding oil price move | Energy supply disruption not yet priced in—potential information edge |

**Geopolitical & Military Signals**

| Signal | Trigger | What It Means |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| **🌍 Geographic Convergence** | 3+ event types in same 1°×1° grid cell | Multiple independent data streams converging on same location—heightened regional activity |
| **🔺 Hotspot Escalation** | Multi-component score exceeds threshold with rising trend | Hotspot showing corroborated escalation across news, CII, convergence, and military data |
| **✈ Military Surge** | Transport/fighter activity 2× baseline in theater | Unusual military airlift concentration—potential deployment or crisis response |

### How It Works

The correlation engine maintains rolling snapshots of:

- News topic frequency (by keyword extraction)
- Market price changes
- Prediction market probabilities

Each refresh cycle compares current state to previous snapshot, applying thresholds and deduplication to avoid alert fatigue. Signals include confidence scores (60-95%) based on the strength of the pattern.

### Signal Deduplication

To prevent alert fatigue, signals use **type-specific TTL (time-to-live)** values for deduplication:

| Signal Type | TTL | Rationale |
|-------------|-----|-----------|
| **Silent Divergence** | 6 hours | Market moves persist; don't re-alert on same stock |
| **Flow-Price Divergence** | 6 hours | Energy events unfold slowly |
| **Explained Market Move** | 6 hours | Same correlation shouldn't repeat |
| **Prediction Leading** | 2 hours | Prediction markets update more frequently |
| **Other signals** | 30 minutes | Default for fast-moving events |

Market signals use **symbol-only keys** (e.g., `silent_divergence:AVGO`) rather than including the price change. This means a stock moving +2.5% then +3.0% won't trigger duplicate alerts—the first alert covers the story.

### Market Alert Ledger

The same market-alert detectors also run server-side every 5 minutes in `seed-market-alert-ledger`, a section of the `seed-bundle-derived-signals` Railway service. The server scores the union of the seeded stock, commodity and crypto quotes, while the dashboard scores only its watchlist, so the alerted symbols can differ between the surfaces. A qualifying emission opens a 6-hour window in the persistent ledger `correlation:market-alerts:ledger:v1`, keyed by signal type, entity and deadline. A repeat emission inside the window increments that window's sample count and opens no second row.

The rolling 30-day scorecard at `correlation:market-alerts:scorecard:v1` reports the hit rate, the base rate and the median lead time per signal type, with the archive read status of the latest resolution pass that read the archive and when it read. `hitRate` counts every resolved HIT or MISS row, while `baseHitRate` and `pairedHitRate` count only the rows whose control scored, so the like-for-like comparison is `pairedHitRate` against `baseHitRate`, not the overall `hitRate`. `medianLeadTimeMs` is the median over HIT rows. `GET /api/forecast/v1/get-forecast-scorecard` and the `get_forecast_scorecard` MCP tool serve the scorecard as `marketAlerts` and omit `medianLeadTimeMs` for a type with fewer than 30 hits, and for `prediction_leads_news` until 30 of its control windows scored. These are the gates the [forecast accuracy scorecard](https://www.worldmonitor.app/accuracy/) applies, and its dataset download withholds the same medians. It carries the resolution rule and the base-rate rule verbatim as `methodology`:

> An emission resolves HIT when a story first tracked by the English news digest within six hours after the emission has at least one Tier 1 or Tier 2 source and its title names the same entity: for a market, an alias match for the symbol's registry entity or a whole-word match of the market name of four or more characters; for a prediction market, a topic keyword that appears in the question itself. It resolves MISS when six hours pass with no such story. It resolves VOID, and is not counted, when the story archive for its window expired before the resolver read it. Lead time is the gap between emission and the matching story's first tracking. The base rate applies the same rule to a control window of the same length for the same entity that starts 24 hours before each scored emission, counted only when the archive covers the control window, no alert for that entity was open during it, and the emission and the control fall on the same side of a weekend in UTC.

A closed window is scored only while the digest archive still holds a story last seen at or before the window opened; an archive that expired and was rebuilt cannot prove that no story appeared, so such windows stay pending and void after six days. A control window is skipped, and not counted in the base rate, when the archive does not cover its start, when its start is older than six days, when any alert for the same entity was open, or still re-emitting while held, or held with no fresh row of its type to bump, during it, or when exactly one of the emission and the control start falls on a Saturday or Sunday in UTC, so a Monday emission is not scored against Sunday news. A row of a given type opens for a symbol only when the previous tick, no more than 15 minutes earlier, carried a quote for the symbol, and no held activity for the symbol is under way that holds the type. Held activity is the symbol's rows, each from its emission to its window close or its last re-emission, whichever is later, and its runs of held alerts; spans no more than 15 minutes apart are one stretch, and a stretch is under way until 15 minutes after it ends. Any stretch under way holds a quiet-market alert (silent divergence, flow/price divergence), so one held move yields at most one row of each quiet type however the detector flips between explained and silent, and a dropout inside another type's window does not reopen the move. A stretch holds an explained alert only when it has already rowed or held one, so a move that was silent and then found its news gets one explained row. After a seeder gap or a cold start, every alert still being emitted stays held; such a hold bumps a fresh row of its type or else records a run naming the type, so the move cannot reopen inside the gap. A seeder outage does not age a move: the ticks the seeder skipped observed nothing and count as one, so a move fresh at the last snapshot is one tick older on the first tick after it, however long the outage lasted. Stock quotes are not refreshed while US equity markets are closed, so a Monday gap-open on a symbol whose quote was stale over the weekend is held until the symbol has a baseline, and a move on a symbol that was already alerted on the previous tick is not distinguishable from that alert. A prediction alert whose question carries no topic keyword opens no row, because the rule could never score it.
