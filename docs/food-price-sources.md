# Official food prices and food-security context

The Food prices panel adds local weekly comparisons to the existing FAO global monthly index. It does not rank countries: baskets, markets, units and currencies differ.

Sources added for #9892 run through per-source adapters in `scripts/lib/food-prices-adapters/`. Each adapter keeps the same publisher-host, size, page and budget limits, and may name extra file hosts in its registry entry. Publisher-specific rules:

- MAGA and UCDA keep no dated archive that the seeder can read for the prior week, and SIMA is polled one bulletin per run at the publisher's request. Their weekly changes come from the seeder's own 14-day history, so they appear after a week of successful runs, and a missed run leaves no baseline for that day.
- OCPV sometimes republishes the previous week's table under a new week. An identical table is not published as a new week.
- Amman cards list highest, most common and lowest prices. Rows that break that order are dropped with a warning; a widespread break fails the run as a layout change. Items without an English name are skipped.
- TCB serves an incomplete certificate chain. Its adapter adds the public Sectigo intermediate for that connection only; certificate verification stays on.
- Kyrgyzstan's workbook is a legacy `.xls` file larger than the download limit. The adapter reads the two newest sheets with byte-range requests.
- Algeria's daily PDFs are discovered from the publisher's WordPress media list, since titles vary (a `-MCIRMN` suffix when the commerce ministry co-signs, or no underscore before the year as in `prix_30_Sept2026`). A row whose mean falls outside its own minimum and maximum fails the run as a layout change.
- Mongolia's weekly tables come from the PXWeb API on data.1212.mn as json-stat2 (www.1212.mn and opendata.1212.mn did not answer our hosts). Each run reads the two newest weeks, so weekly changes appear from the first run; a week that is not seven days before the newest is dropped. If the aimag table's newest week differs from the capital table's, the run fails and the last complete snapshot stays published.
- Chile's prices come from the CKAN datastore on datos.odepa.gob.cl (the datos.gob.cl mirror returns 404). Each year is a separate resource of the dataset, found by its `año YYYY` name. A run reads the newest week and the Monday seven days earlier, filtered to the basket's product labels; at the turn of the year the earlier week comes from the previous year's resource, and a new year's resource with no rows yet falls back to the previous year. A partial page, a week start that is not a Monday, a mean outside its own minimum and maximum, or fewer than 80% of the basket labels fails the run as a format change. Fruit and onion labels carry cultivar and season, so the basket keeps cultivars ODEPA lists all year, and each onion season label (new, stored, harvest) is its own series.
- Canada's prices come from the Statistics Canada Web Data Service: the table's metadata names the geographies and products, and the newest month of every geography and food product is read in batches of 300 coordinates, the service's limit. StatCan's edge refuses Railway's egress, so the adapter goes through the proxy. A geography that does not collect a product answers with no series and is left out; a suppressed or null cell is never published as a price. A moved dimension, an unknown unit label, a short batch or a national table with fewer than half the foods fails the run.
- Brazil's IPCA comes from the SIDRA API (apisidra.ibge.gov.br) and links the table page on sidra.ibge.gov.br. The run reads the newest month only; a relabelled group, a missing value, a unit other than percent or mixed months fail the run.
- Uzbekistan data is CC BY 4.0 and must link to www.stat.uz. SIMA asks for the citation "SIMA Niger" and contact before commercial use or mass extraction. Amman's site states all rights reserved. MADR and the Mongolia NSO do not state a reuse licence; both are credited by name. ODEPA publishes the dataset under Creative Commons Attribution and is credited by name. Statistics Canada data is used under the Statistics Canada Open Licence, which requires the source credit. IBGE's licence pages did not answer our checks, so IBGE is credited by name and its terms stay under review. Each registry entry records the requested attribution.

Report discovery follows publisher links. The PBS archive's annex date is authoritative, since post slugs can differ from the report date. PDF dates must agree with discovery dates. PDF layout checks, bounded response sizes and same-publisher HTTPS redirects prevent a changed or blocked document from becoming price data. Mexico requests the most recent completed weekday and the same date seven days earlier; missing holiday quotes stay absent. Paginated SNIIM responses fail rather than silently truncate.

## Storage and failure behavior

Run `node scripts/seed-food-prices-<suffix>.mjs`. Each script uses `loadEnvFile()` and `runSeed()` and writes its own `economic:food-prices:<suffix>:v1`, `seed-meta:economic:food-prices-<suffix>` and activation marker. The macro bundle schedules each source as an independent job. Railway watch patterns include the entrypoints, shared registry and parser/fetch/seed helpers. No new credential is required.

A successful run merges at most 14 calendar days of history with the new observations. A strict prior-cache read prevents an unavailable Redis read from erasing a baseline. `runSeed()` retains last-good data on collection or validation failure; it does not advance the successful fetch clock. Individual GIEWS failures retain that country's prior dated brief and fetch clock, with an explicit failed-fetch flag. An undated brief has unavailable status. Each source has separate fetch-age and report-age limits in `shared/food-price-sources.json`. Health probes seed metadata rather than downloading history, and becomes strict after the first successful publish.

`GetFoodPrices` reads every source cache in one pipeline and makes no source requests. The latest source report's prices are compared only against the exact same market, market type, item, origin, unit and currency seven calendar days earlier. Malaysia's PriceCatcher basket-survey items (packaged rice, cooking oil, sugar, flour, coconut, meat and fruit, listed as `surveyItems` in the source registry) are quoted only on irregular survey days, so each shows its own latest survey date and is compared with its most recent survey 7 to 28 days earlier (no comparison when there is none); `previousDate` states the baseline. A missing baseline has `wowAvailable: false`; its numeric default is never a displayed zero change. Dropped items are not carried forward into the latest report. Tunisia, Uzbekistan and GIEWS never receive weekly changes. Country briefs have independent freshness status. Any incomplete/stale result is marked non-cacheable.

## Verification and proof limits

The fixtures under `tests/fixtures/food-prices/` are compact extracts of official reports fetched on 2026-10-29. PDF fixtures preserve text positions. They cover PIHPS 2026-10-15 through 29, PBS 2026-10-22, DA/CBSL 2026-10-29, SNIIM 2026-10-21, ONAGRI July 2026, and the Bangladesh GIEWS brief of 2026-09-04. The `madr.json` and `nsomn.json` fixtures were fetched on 2026-11-02: MADR reports for 2026-11-02 and 2026-10-26 with the media list, and NSO json-stat2 for the weeks of 2026-10-26 and 2026-10-19. `odepa.json` was fetched on 2026-11-26: the dataset's newest yearly resources and the basket rows of the weeks starting 2026-11-16 and 2026-11-09 for the Oriente and online sectors of the Metropolitan region and Punilla in Ñuble. `statcan.json` was fetched on 2026-11-26: the table metadata and the September 2026 series for Canada, Ontario and Yellowknife. `ibge.json` is the SIDRA response for October 2026. `odepa-card.json` is the reader's response built from that extract for the browser test; it shows rendering, not production data.

Parser, seed-merge, transport-boundary, handler and DOM tests exercise date identity, unit identity, missing prices, exact-week baselines, partial context, freshness, source switching and safe text rendering. Live read-only collection on 2026-10-29 succeeded for each adapter available at that time (MADR and NSO were collected on 2026-11-02); the GIEWS catalog had undated briefs (Malaysia and Saudi Arabia). This does not prove production Redis publication, Railway scheduling, deployment or production acceptance. Those require separately authorized operations and natural-run evidence.
